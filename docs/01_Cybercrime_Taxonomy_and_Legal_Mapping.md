> The case is fictional. The following is an academic classification of conduct, not a charging decision.

## 1. Crimes in the scenario

1. ATM skimming and unauthorised capture of card information.
2. Card cloning using captured magnetic-stripe information.
3. Online/card-not-present fraud using stolen credentials.
4. Possession and sale of stolen card data.
5. Unauthorised access/interception of computer data.
6. Coordinated activity between multiple actors.
7. Movement of criminal proceeds through digital assets.

## 2. Indian legal mapping

| Conduct | Relevant provision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorised access or extraction of computer data | IT Act s.43 and s.66 | Covers unauthorised acts done dishonestly or fraudulently. |
| Fraudulent use of another person's identifying credentials | IT Act s.66C | Addresses identity theft and use of another person's unique identification feature. |
| Cheating through a computer resource/personation | IT Act s.66D | Relevant to online impersonation and computer-enabled cheating. |
| Receiving or retaining stolen computer resources/devices | IT Act s.66B | Relevant where the device/data is known to be stolen. |
| Cheating and dishonest delivery of property | IPC s.420 / current BNS equivalent should be checked for present proceedings | Relevant to the fictional financial loss. |
| Criminal conspiracy | IPC s.120B / current BNS equivalent should be checked | Relevant to coordinated conduct. |

The assignment uses the IPC terminology for the historical mapping. For an actual investigation today, the applicable BNS provisions and current procedural law must be checked.

## 3. International aspect

The fictional case includes an India–UAE link. International evidence would normally require the appropriate current mutual legal assistance, preservation and law-enforcement channels. The Budapest Convention should not be treated as automatically applicable to India simply because it is discussed in cybercrime literature.
